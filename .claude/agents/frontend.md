## Must deliver

0a. **Brand (product owner, 2026-10-11):** the site is called **Nova** (renamed from Shoe Rental, product owner 2026-10-12), and UI text says "shoes", never "sneakers". The only website colours are pure white (#FFFFFF), pure black (#000000) and one blue, **#35D6ED**: tokens in `src/app/globals.css` (`paper`, `ink`, `accent`, `on-accent`; greys are black or white at low opacity; `good` / `warn` / `danger` / `info` map onto black or white). The blue is a fill (primary buttons, the sign-up button, search button, good badges, the logo mark, the price chart) with black text on it (`text-on-accent`); never blue text on white (contrast too low). Status shows by shape, not colour: good = blue pill, problem = black pill or black-outlined alert, warning = outlined. Don't add another colour. Photos and emoji icons are content, not palette. **Day and night mode:** every colour token is `light-dark(day, night)` in `globals.css`; the page follows the device setting until the visitor uses the header switch (`ThemeToggle`, sun/moon, "Switch to night mode" / "Switch to day mode"), which sets `data-theme` on `<html>` and a `theme` cookie (1 year) that the root layout reads, so the server renders the picked mode with no flash. Night mode is black background, white text, the same blue. New colours go in as `light-dark()` tokens, never as `dark:` classes.
0. **Password fields** (sign-in, sign-up, and any later one) use `PasswordInput` (`src/components/password-input.tsx`): an eye button at the right of the field shows or hides what was typed ("Show password" / "Hide password", `aria-pressed`). Never a bare `type="password"` input. (Product owner, 2026-10-11.)
0b. **Forgot password (product owner, 2026-10-11):** "Forgot password?" on sign-in → `/forgot-password` emails a one-time link (`requestPasswordReset`, `src/server/auth/password-reset.ts`) valid 60 minutes, at most one email per account a minute; the page says the same thing whether or not the email has an account. `/reset-password?token=` sets the new password (`resetPassword`): the link and every other open link stop working and all sessions are signed out; then sign-in shows "Your password has been changed". Only the token's SHA-256 is stored (`PasswordReset`). Links use `APP_URL`, never the request's Host header. With the mock mailer outside production, the page shows the link, since no email really goes out.
0c. **Sizes (product owner, 2026-10-11):** a merchant lists a pair in the size on its label, **UK, EU or US** (`Shoe.sizeSystem` + number; `size` is the label, e.g. "EU 39"), and we store the same size in every system, `sizeUk` / `sizeEu` / `sizeUs` / `sizeCm` (cm = foot length), from the product owner's charts in `src/lib/shoe-size.ts` (`shoeSizeFields`): women's pairs use the women's chart, men's and unisex the men's. Kids' pairs and sizes off the chart keep only the listed system (no kids' chart yet). Changing who a pair is for re-reads its label size on the new chart. Borrowers search in any system (`?size=UK 6`, `EU 39`, `US 8.5`, `24.5 cm`) and find a pair however it was listed; a size search shows what that size is in the other systems on each chart (`SizeEquivalence`), cards show the listed size plus the borrower's system (`sizeSummary`, e.g. "EU 39 · UK 6"), the listing shows all four (`ShoeSizePanel`), and `/size-guide` shows both charts with the size highlighted. Never edit the charts without the product owner. The Add shoe form asks **Size system** (UK / EU / US) and **Size** (`SizeFields`, suggestions from the chart for who it's for, and a live line saying what borrowers will also see); Size filters on home and browse group the options by system (`SizeOptions`), and "Size guide" links sit by the filters, on the listing and in the form.
0d. **Wording: "per day", never "wear-day" (product owner, 2026-10-12).** Everything a visitor, borrower, merchant or admin reads says "per day" for prices (`LKR 28.92 per day`, "per day today", "Price per day", "LKR per day") and "days" for counts and lifespans ("120 of 365 days used", "Expected lifespan (days)", "Wear already used (days)", chart table "Days worn"). This covers pages, components, form errors, API errors and notices (the server's messages too). "Wear-day" stays only in code comments, the engine and the specs.
0e. **Listings (product owner, 2026-10-12):** **Brand and model are optional** ("Brand (optional)", "Model (optional)", not `required`). Always show a pair's name with `shoeName(shoe)` (`src/lib/shoe-name.ts`): brand and model, or "Unbranded shoes" when both are empty; never write `${brand} ${model}` by hand. **Experiences are a multi-select:** `StyleFields` shows the audience's experiences as checkbox chips named `experiences` (pick one or more; changing the audience drops picks it doesn't offer); `styleLabel` shows them joined ("Women · Work Day, Night Out"). Server actions read the form with `formFields(formData)` so repeated `experiences` survive. The Payouts page (`/merchant/payouts`) has no description under its title (no "paid weekly … Paid to …" sentence).
1. **Merchant**
   - Sign-up, business verification and payout account. Sign-up asks every customer and merchant for their **one address** ("Delivery and collection address" / "Pickup and return address") with the reason under it, then their **City** (a separate required field, both account types); a customer is also asked for their **Age** (whole years, a number field after Email; hidden for merchants), and their Account page shows it (`ADDRESS_LABEL` / `ADDRESS_REASON` in `src/lib/address.ts`). Account pages (`/account` for customers, linked as "Account" in their nav; `/merchant/account`) let them change address and city with `AddressForm`, which says trips booked from then on use it. The listing form has no address field: it shows "Picked up from and returned to" with the account address and a link to change it. The sign-up form offers exactly two account types, shown as **Customer** (a borrower) and **Merchant**, with no "I want to" prompt (product owner, 2026-10-11). Use "Customer", not "buyer", in UI text for this choice.
   - Add shoe: 5 required photos (front, side, sole, inside, box), V, L (6–24 months, or the merchant's admin-granted maximum; say how to ask for longer), a **Condition** select (Brand new / Second-hand, no upgrades / Second-hand, upgraded; labels from `src/lib/shoe-condition.ts`), the wear already used shown and required only for second-hand pairs, and "What was upgraded" shown and required only for upgraded pairs, size, brand and model (both optional), **Who it's for** (Men / Women / Unisex (men and women) / Boys / Girls) and **Experiences** (one or more checkbox chips; the list follows the audience; `StyleFields`, lists in `src/lib/shoe-style.ts`), pickup address. A **Pricing method** choice with a **"?" after the heading** (product owner, 2026-10-12: a round button, "How to choose a pricing method", whose tooltip shows on hover, tap and keyboard focus and hides on Escape; text from `PRICING_METHOD_HELP` in `src/lib/pricing-method.ts`: pick the curve that matches how this pair wears, because its price should fall the way its quality does: S-curve for pairs that stay like new for a good while then wear faster in mid-life, exponential decay for pairs that lose their fresh look early then wear slowly) (`PricingMethodFields`: radio cards "S-curve (logistic decay)" (default) and "Exponential decay" with one-line descriptions from `src/lib/pricing-method.ts`, and a hint that both end at the floor (q, from settings, as a %) and earn the same over the pair's life); the live curve preview follows the chosen method (`&method=` on the preview API). The merchant's pair page has a "Who it's for" card to change both at any time, a pricing-method card **only while the pair is a draft** (`PricingMethodForm`, "Save pricing method"), and names the method under "Price curve". The public listing names the method ("Exponential decay, picked by the merchant") in its price-curve card. Never describe exponential decay as "earning sooner": the S-curve collects more in the first half of life; exponential is dearer when brand new and when well worn, cheaper in between. The pair's page shows "Condition when listed" (with the upgrades).
   - **Curve preview before publish** (§5.1): chart of P(w) over 0..L, plus start price, end price and lifetime income R and merchant share, all from the API. Note that a longer L lowers the daily price but risks uncollected income.
   - Dashboard: every pair with status, committed wear / L ("used, counting current bookings"), collected so far, idle days and upcoming payouts.
   - Returns: pairs to inspect, pairs being cleaned, and pairs ended but not back yet ("Collection requested", "Going to the next borrower", or "Waiting with the borrower for the next booking").
2. **Borrower**
   - **Home (`/`)**: an experience-led discovery home (product owner, 2026-10-11/13) guiding customers through moments and cohesive rotations rather than a plain shoe catalog. Features:
     - Shop-by tabs (All · Men · Women · Boys · Girls) and rounded search pill submitting to `/browse`.
     - **Curate by Moment & Occasion** (`OccasionDiscovery` in `src/components/occasion-discovery.tsx`): 4 photo-driven cards (*Morning Run & Performance*, *The Daily Commute & Work Day*, *Weekend Stroll & Brunch*, *Night Out & Big Occasions*) with frosted glass icons and bold titles over authentic lifestyle photography.
     - **Curated Rotations & Style Packs** (`CuratedPacksShowcase` in `src/components/curated-packs.tsx`): pre-assembled wardrobe rotations highlighting the Wear-Sharing Engine (*The Marathon & Active Recovery*, *The All-Week Urban Wardrobe*, *The 24/7 City Explorer*).
     - Sideways scrolling rows (`ShoeRow`): **Exceptional Value first** (ordered by discount, highest first; subtitle: **"Unmatched Value, From Day One"**), then Just listed, Best value today, Brand new pairs, Pre-loved and upgraded, Being worn now: reserve next.
     - "How it works" strip explaining that borrowers can hold **any number of pairs** and wear is split across them.
   - **Home search bar (product owner, 2026-10-12):** "Size" (dropdown starting at "Any size") and "Brand or model" (keep that label) with a **suggestions dropdown** (`SearchBox`, `src/components/search-box.tsx`): as the visitor types, matching brands and models (`suggestShoes`, `GET /api/shoes/suggest?q=`, public, brands first, a brand's models too) drop down under the bar; arrow keys, Enter and Escape work; picking one searches Browse for it.
   - **Header for signed-out visitors (product owner, 2026-10-12):** the day/night switch, **Sign in** and **Sign up** only. There is no "Browse" link there; visitors shop from the home page. Signed-in customers keep "Browse" in their menu.
   - **Browse (`/browse`)**: titled **"Elevate Your Everyday"**. Cards are kept clean and focused for customers: Model Name, One Size, Rent Price per day, and Condition badge (with uniform `DiscountBadge` 80×24px). Extra technical clutter is removed from cards. Filter by size, brand, and experience.
   - **Bundle builder (`/bundle`)**: borrowers can hold **any number of pairs** they want at once (no 4-pair cap). Term in days (1–365): per pair the wear it starts and ends at, the expected days (day 1 = the day after delivery) and its rent; the bundle's daily cost (1/n split explained: "Hold as many pairs as you want at once. Each day's wear is split across all pairs you hold, keeping your daily cost balanced"); "Rent estimate for N days, locked now"; "Deposit: pair values, locked while you rent"; tokens needed against available tokens.
   - Checkout (locks deposits and rent estimate, charges nothing, books delivery; no address field: shows account address and change link; disabled without one). Borrowers pay the driver directly in the app.
   - Wallet (`/wallet`): available / locked / total tokens, top up by card, withdraw to card (available tokens only: deposits and rent estimates stay locked while pairs are out), history.
   - My rentals: per pair the term's days (expected days for a reservation), planned wear, the rent estimate locked (the charge once closed), and the tokens locked; **Return early** (a date picker limited to the range the API gives: from today + notice days to the day before the term ends; say the unused days' estimate is unlocked at once), shown "Early return booked: last day X"; **Extend** (days, estimate locked; hidden once someone has reserved next); **End rental** only when the API allows it (free-return window or the term's last day); an offer with the explanation (new day 1 vs the original, less wear than shown, old vs new estimate, answer by date-time, declining or keeping the original dates cancels the reservation and unlocks its tokens) and **Accept new dates and price** / **No, cancel my reservation**; "please keep the pair safe" while the next borrower decides, or, for a `holdForNext` pair, until the next borrower books it ("you aren't charged for these days"). A held pair whose release day has passed shows "Free now" in Browse and on its page.
   - Notices (`/notifications`, linked from the header with the unread count): in-app notices (offers of new dates and price, cancelled reservations, unlocked tokens, term ends), mark all read. The same notices are emailed by the server.

## Must not

- Compute any price, charge, share or wear in the browser or in server components. Display what the API returns. The curve preview data comes from the API.
- Round anything except for display (2 dp, LKR).
- Call payment or logistics providers directly.
